# Research Method: From Rare Idea to Evidence

## 1. Name the observation

Record what was directly seen or measured before explaining it. Keep the raw value, unit, timestamp, instrument, calibration state, environment, and operator or software version.

## 2. Define the claim

A useful claim identifies:

- the proposed cause;
- the observable effect;
- the expected magnitude and time window;
- the conditions under which it should occur;
- a result that would count against it.

Words such as *resonance*, *coherence*, *energy*, *frequency*, and *quantum* must map to measurable quantities. A metaphor is welcome in the idea notebook but is not yet a mechanism.

## 3. Build the null model

Explain the result expected without the proposed mechanism. Include ordinary thermal drift, vibration, contamination, electromagnetic interference, humidity, software artifacts, selection effects, and random variation where relevant.

## 4. Design controls

Use a non-hazardous low-energy design first. A strong comparison may include:

- a visually identical sham or no-material control;
- randomized sample labels;
- blinded analysis;
- positive and negative controls;
- repeated calibration checks;
- a preregistered analysis and detection threshold.

## 5. Keep the evidence ladder visible

An attractive plot, unusual color, or single successful run is an observation. It does not by itself demonstrate a mechanism. Move a claim upward only after controls, repeatability, uncertainty analysis, and independent replication.

## 6. Separate the robots

- **Watcher:** gathers records and preserves source provenance.
- **Modeler:** defines variables, null models, and predictions.
- **Tester:** drafts safe protocols and stop conditions.
- **Archivist:** publishes raw data, uncertainty, hashes, and readable reports.

No research agent may silently promote a claim’s evidence class. Every promotion requires a visible evidence record and human review.

## Boron-oxide question template

For a proposed B₂O₃ device, answer these before building:

1. Which phase, composition, geometry, and preparation state is intended?
2. What physical state represents information?
3. How is that state written and read?
4. What calibrated instrument observes it?
5. What is the predicted signal, uncertainty, and energy budget?
6. Which sham material controls for heat, capacitance, optics, and geometry?
7. What result would disprove or pause the mechanism?
8. Can a separate laboratory reproduce it from the published record?

## Safety boundary

The portal excludes instructions for hazardous synthesis, reactive-material handling, radiation production, high voltage, high pressure, autonomous chemical delivery, and uncontrolled heating. Escalate any such work to qualified laboratory and engineering review.
